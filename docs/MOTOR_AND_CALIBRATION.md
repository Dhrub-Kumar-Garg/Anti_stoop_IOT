# Coin motor and one-tap calibration

## Identify the motor first

These instructions assume a **two-wire brushed ERM coin motor** and the **ULN2003A driver** referenced in the sketch. Check the motor's rated voltage and current before choosing a supply. A two-wire LRA actuator needs a suitable haptic driver and is not interchangeable with an ERM motor driven by DC.

Do not connect a bare motor to an ESP32 GPIO. The GPIO is a control signal; a driver switches motor current. Never apply 5 V to an ESP32 GPIO or ADC input. Keep the wiring insulated and test off the body before fitting the vest.

## ULN2003A low-side wiring

| Connection | Connect to |
| --- | --- |
| ESP32 GPIO 32 | Driver IN1 |
| Motor positive | Motor supply positive, at the motor's rated voltage |
| Motor negative | Driver OUT1 |
| Driver GND | ESP32 GND and motor-supply negative |
| Driver COM / clamp common | Motor-supply positive |

For the bare 16-pin ULN2003A package, IN1 is pin 1, OUT1 is pin 16, GND is pin 8, and COM is pin 9. On a module, use its labeled terminals and schematic: motor connector positions, power jumpers, and exposed outputs vary. COM is the flyback-diode common connection, not a logic power input.

The ULN2003A uses Darlington outputs and has a significant on-state voltage drop (roughly a volt or more, depending on current). A low-voltage coin motor may receive too little voltage to start even when the board's indicator LED lights. Measure the voltage **across the motor during an ON pulse** and check against its datasheet. Do not compensate by exceeding the motor's rated voltage; a suitably rated logic-level MOSFET driver specified at a 3.3 V gate signal may be a better fit. Confirm its flyback protection and power ratings too.

If using a transistor/MOSFET driver without a built-in clamp, a correctly rated flyback diode goes across the motor: cathode/striped end toward motor positive, anode toward the switched motor negative. Use a supply with sufficient startup current. Whether the ESP32 board's regulator can supply that current depends on the board and motor; do not assume its 3.3 V pin has spare capacity.

Sources: [TI ULN2003A datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/uln2003a.pdf), [Adafruit motor-driver example](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-arduino-lesson-13-dc-motors?view=all).

## Isolate a motor fault

1. Confirm the motor is an ERM type and briefly test it from a supply at its rated voltage, disconnected from the driver. If it does not spin, check the supply, contacts, and motor before changing firmware.
2. With power disconnected, verify the driver wiring and common ground above.
3. Upload the updated sketch and let Wi-Fi startup finish. The original sketch's Wi-Fi wait remains in setup, so neither button is processed until setup completes.
4. Wire a normally open test button between **GPIO 14 and GND**. Tap and release. The red LED and GPIO 32 driver signal should turn on for **300 ms**. Serial Monitor at 115200 prints `Motor test: GPIO 32 driver pulse for 300 ms.` No posture alert is generated by this test.
5. If the red LED responds but the motor does not, measure driver input and motor voltage during repeated test taps. Check that IN1 controls the OUT1 used by the motor. A driver LED alone is not proof of motor current.
6. If the ESP32 resets when the motor starts, inspect supply capacity, grounding, and driver/flyback wiring. Do not continue a body-worn test with unstable power.

A four-leg tactile switch typically has internally connected pairs. Use contacts that are open at rest and close only when pressed, verified with continuity mode. A permanently grounded GPIO reads as a held button and cannot generate another tap until released.

## Fit and calibrate

1. Secure each flex sensor so it bends consistently with the intended upper/lower back movement. A loose sensor moving independently of the wearer cannot give repeatable posture feedback. Prevent sharp folds and follow its mechanical bend limits.
2. Fit the vest snugly enough to avoid slipping while remaining comfortable. Keep the motor mechanically secured so its vibration can be felt without exposed wiring touching skin.
3. Sit or stand in your comfortable neutral posture. Do not force an exaggerated straight-back position.
4. Tap the **GPIO 27 → GND** calibration button once and release it. Keep still for about **0.5 seconds**. The firmware averages 50 readings with the motor off, then gives a **200 ms** confirmation pulse. The UI displays capture progress and the reported new baseline.
5. Check Serial Monitor's `delta` readings in neutral, during normal small movements, and during a repeatable forward bend. Hold a bend beyond threshold for over **1 second**: the alert LED/motor should activate. Return within threshold: the posture alert should clear.
6. Recalibrate after repositioning sensors, changing wearer, or changing vest fit. Baselines are in RAM; boot uses the original automatic baseline, so tap again after fitting the vest following a reset.

The current sensitive test setting is **100 ADC counts for 1 second**, reduced from 500 counts for 5 seconds. Calibrate after fitting the vest, then check neutral jitter and bend deltas in Serial Monitor. These settings are intended to make bench/wearer testing easier and may react to small movements. If the sensor change never exceeds 100 counts, inspect its mounting, voltage divider, and measured range rather than assuming calibration has failed. Adjust sensitivity based on repeatable wearer measurements; no automatic threshold learning is implemented. Flex sensors measure their own bending, not an anatomical spinal angle.
